## What "purity" actually measures

When a research compound is listed at **≥98% purity**, that figure almost always
refers to *chromatographic* purity measured by HPLC — the percentage of the total
signal area attributable to the target molecule. It is a statement about the sample
that was tested, not a guarantee about every vial ever produced.

Purity and **identity** are different questions. Purity asks "how much of this is the
intended molecule?" Identity asks "is this the intended molecule at all?" A complete
Certificate of Analysis (COA) addresses both.

## How to read a COA

A useful COA typically reports:

- **Identity** — confirmed by mass spectrometry (MS), matching the expected molecular weight.
- **Purity** — by HPLC, with the percentage and often the chromatogram.
- **Batch / lot number** — ties the document to a specific production run.
- **Test date** and the testing method.

> A COA describes the batch that was tested. Always match the lot number on the
> document to the lot you are referencing.

## Questions worth asking

1. Does the reported molecular weight match the published value for this peptide?
2. Is the purity method stated (HPLC), and is a chromatogram included?
3. Does the lot number on the COA match the material in question?
