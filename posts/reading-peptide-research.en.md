## Start with the source

Not all "studies" carry the same weight. A rough hierarchy, strongest to weakest:
peer-reviewed clinical trials, peer-reviewed animal studies, *in vitro* (cell) work,
preprints, and finally anecdote. Most peptide literature sits in the preclinical
range — interesting signals, not settled conclusions.

## Read the model, not just the headline

A finding in a petri dish or a rodent is a starting hypothesis, not a human result.
When you read a claim, ask **what system it came from**:

- *In vitro* — isolated cells; no whole-body context.
- *In vivo* (animal) — a living system, but not a human one.
- Human trials — the only model that speaks directly to people, and the rarest.

## Watch for limitations

- **Sample size** — a handful of animals is hypothesis-generating, not definitive.
- **Dose and route** — preclinical doses rarely translate directly.
- **Conflicts and funding** — note who ran and paid for the work.

> Strong language ("proven", "miracle") is a signal to slow down, not speed up.
